Course Title Linear Algebra & Matrix Analysis Course Code MA01
Department CSE/ECE/DSAI/SCI. & MATHS/HUMANITIES Credits, Structure ( L- 3, (2-1-0-0)

### & MGMT. T-P-S)

Offered for All Programs Type Compulsory
Prerequisite NA Laboratory No
Course Outcomes
At the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Solve systems of linear equations using multiple methods, including Gaussian elimination and
matrix inversion.

- Carry out matrix operations, including inverses and determinants.
- Determine eigenvalues and eigenvectors and solve eigenvalue problems.
- Apply principles of matrix algebra to linear transformations.
- Demonstrate understanding of inner products and associated norms.
Contents:
System of linear equations, Inverse and rank of a matrix by using elementary transformations,
Properties of determinants
Vector space over real and complex fields, Linear dependence, independence and spanning set,
Linear transformation and its matrix, Change of basis, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
Diagonalization of matrices.
Inner product spaces, Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization, Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).
Text Books

- Seymour Lipschutz, Marc Lipson, Schaum’s outlines of Linear Algebra ,McGraw-Hill Education
- J. Defranza and D. Gagliardi, Introduction to Linear Algebra with Applications, McGraw-Hill
- K. Hoffman and R. Kunze, Linear Algebra, Prentice Hall.
- Strang, Gilbert. Introduction to Linear Algebra. 4th ed. Wellesley-Cambridge Press ,2009.

### Course Title Calculus Course Code MA02

Department CSE/ECE/DSAI/SCI. & Credits, Structure ( L-T- 3, (2-1-0-0)

### MATHS/HUMANITIES & MGMT. P-S)

Offered for All Programs Type Compulsory
Prerequisite NA Laboratory No
Course Outcomes
At the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Find limits of functions (graphically, numerically and algebraically)
- Analyse and apply the notions of continuity and differentiability to algebraic and
transcendental functions.

- Determine derivatives using implicit differentiation and use to study characteristics of a curve.
- Apply the Fundamental theorem of calculus to evaluate definite integrals.
- Use differentiation and integration to solve real world problems such as rate of change,
optimization, and area problems.
Contents:
Review of single variable calculus: Review of Limit, continuity and differentiability of single variable
functions, Indeterminate forms and L’Hospital rule, Mean Value theorem, Maclaurin and Taylor
series expansions of functions of one variable.
Functions of Several variables: Functions of several variables, Limits and continuity, Partial
derivatives and differentiability, Linearization and differentials, Chain rule, Gradient vector, Tangent
planes, Directional derivatives, Extreme values and saddle points, Lagrange multipliers, Taylor’s
formula, Partial derivatives with constrained variables.
Multiple integral: Multiple integral, Double integrals, Change of order of integration, Area and
volume by double integral, Double integrals in polar form, Triple integrals in rectangular
coordinates, Triple integrals in cylindrical and spherical coordinates, Substitutions in multiple
integrals.
Text Books

- M. D. Weir and J. Hass, Thomas’ Calculus, 12 th edition, Pearson.
- G. B. Thomas and R. L. Finney, Calculus and Analytic Geometry, 9th Ed, Pearson.
- Huges-Hallett et al, Calculus: Single and Multivariable, 6 th edition, John-Wiley & Sons (USA).
- J. Stewart, Multivariable Calculus, Hybrid Edition.

### Course Title Digital Electronics using Course Code ECE01

Verilog
Department CSE/ECE/DSAI/SCI. & Credits, Structure ( L-T- 3, (2-0-2-0)

### MATHS/HUMANITIES & MGMT. P-S)

Offered for All Programs Type Compulsory
Prerequisite NA Laboratory Choice Mandatory 2 Hours
Course Outcomes
At the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Understand the basics of any digital systems such as number system, logic gates, Boolean
logic simplification, FFs etc.

- Understand basic digital system design using standard ICs
- Analyze and design combinational and sequential circuits using above concepts.
- Perform simple course projects using above design technique
Contents:
Number system and Logic Gates, Truth tables of different logic gates. Boolean Algebra and K-maps,
NAND/NOR implementations, Logic Simplification using Tabular method, etc, Combinational Circuit
Design: Design procedure, Different Adders and Subtractors-Half Adder, Full Adder, Half Subtractor,
Full Subtractor, 4-bit Ripple Carry Adder, Carry Look Ahead Adder, Decoder, Encoders, Multiplexers,
De-Multiplexers, Magnitude Comparator, etc. Sequential Circuit Design: Basics, Latches and Flip-
flops, conversion from one FF to another, Serial and Parallel shift Registers, Synchronous and
Asynchronous Counter Designing, Mealy and Moore Machine.
Text Books

- R.P. Jain, “Modern Digital Electronics”, 3rd Edition, Tata McGraw Hill, 2003.
- T.L. Floyd, “Digital Fundamentals”, 10th Edition, Pearson education, 2011
- M. Morris Mano, Michael D. Ciletti, “Digital Design- with an Introduction to the Verilog HDL”,
5th Ed, 2013, Pearson.

### MATHS/HUMANITIES & MGMT. T-P-S)

Course Outcomes
At the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Communicate effectively in the foreign language in a variety of speaking situations.
- Communicate effectively in the foreign language via proficient, articulate, and well-organized
writing.

situations.

- Demonstrate comprehension of a wide range of foreign language written materials.

to Communication, Understanding the Basics of Communication, Role and importance of

IT Workshop: Full Stack Prototyping with Course Code SK01

### Course Title

AI Support
Department CSE/ECE/DSAI/SCI. & MATHS/HUMANITIES Credits, Structure ( L- 2, (1-0-2-2)

### & MGMT. T-P-[C])

Offered for All Programs Type Compulsory
Prerequisite NA Laboratory Choice Mandatory
Course Description: This hands-on workshop enables students to design, develop, and deploy end-to-end functional
prototypes for web and mobile applications, leveraging AI-powered tools for faster development. The course blends
Full Stack development (front-end, back-end, and database) with AI-assisted features such as code generation, UI
design, data handling, and intelligent services.
Course Outcomes (COs): On successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
 CO1: Apply fundamental concepts of full stack development (frontend, backend, database, APIs) to
design functional prototypes.
 CO2: Use AI-assisted tools to accelerate coding, interface design, and backend logic development.
 CO3: Integrate AI/ML services (e.g., Chatbots, Recommendation Engines, Vision APIs) into full stack
prototypes.
 CO4: Deploy prototypes to cloud platforms for public access.
 CO5: Collaborate effectively in teams, managing workflows using modern DevOps tools and agile
principles.
Mapping to Graduate Attributes: Engineering knowledge, Problem Analysis, Design/Development, Modern Tool
Usage, Ethics, Communication, and Project Management.
Course Contents:
Module 1 – Introduction & Tools Setup (Week 1) - Full Stack architecture overview (Client–Server–Database), AI-
powered development tools: GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT, Cursor, Replit, etc., Setting up environment: Node.js,
Python/Flask/Django, React/Vue, VS Code, Git/GitHub
Module 2 – Frontend Development with AI Assistance (Weeks 2–3) - HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript ES6+ basics
(AI-assisted code snippets), Component-based UI design using React/Vue, Responsive design frameworks
(Bootstrap/Tailwind CSS), AI-assisted prototyping for UI/UX
Module 3 – Backend Development & APIs (Weeks 4–5) - REST API concepts and design, Backend frameworks:
Express.js / Flask / Django, AI-assisted code scaffolding for CRUD operations, Authentication & authorization basics
Module 4 – Databases & Integration (Week 6) - SQL (MySQL/PostgreSQL) and NoSQL (MongoDB), AI-assisted
query generation and optimization, ORM integration (SQLAlchemy, Mongoose)
Module 5 – AI/ML Service Integration (Weeks 7–8) - Integrating OpenAI, Hugging Face, or Google Cloud AI APIs,
Adding chatbots, summarizers, sentiment analysis, or computer vision to prototypes, Data preprocessing using AI
tools
Module 6 – Deployment & DevOps Basics (Week 9) - Deploying applications using Vercel, Netlify, or Heroku,
Containerization with Docker (optional), AI-assisted CI/CD setup
Module 7 – Project Sprint & Demo Day (Weeks 10–12) - Agile project management tools, (Trello/Jira), Team sprints
with AI-assisted task completion, Final demo and peer evaluation
Course Projects (Examples): Students will work in teams to develop one full stack prototype with AI-assisted
features. Possible project ideas include:

- AI-Powered Resume Builder – Generates resumes using user input, with real-time formatting and
download options.

- E-Commerce Portal with AI Recommendations – AI-based product suggestions and chatbot support.
- Smart Student Portal – With AI-assisted assignment summaries and automated FAQs.
- Travel Itinerary Planner – AI-generated itineraries based on preferences, with map integration.
- Healthcare Symptom Checker – Uses NLP models to provide probable condition information.
Text Books:

- Srinivasa K G, et al., AI-Assisted Programming for Web and Machine Learning, BPB Publishers, 2025.
- Bates, B., & Downey, C., Programming with AI Tools: A Developer’s Guide, Manning Publications.
References:
 OpenAI API Documentation – https://platform.openai.com/docs
 Hugging Face API Documentation – https://huggingface.co/docs
 Mozilla Developer Network (MDN) – https://developer.mozilla.org/
 Docker Documentation – https://docs.docker.com/
 Tailwind CSS Docs – https://tailwindcss.com/docs
 GitHub Copilot Guide – https://docs.github.com/copilot

### Course Title Entrepreneurship and Design Course Code MGM01

Thinking
Department CSE/ECE/DSAI/SCI. & Credits, Structure ( L- 2, (1-0-2-2)

### MATHS/HUMANITIES & MGMT. T-P-[C])

Offered for All Programs Type Compulsory
Prerequisite NA Laboratory Choice Faculty Choice
Course Outcomes
At the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Identify the importance of entrepreneurship.
- To familiarize students with design thinking concepts and principles
- To ensure students can practice the methods, processes and tools of design thinking.
- To ensure students can apply the design thinking approach and have ability to model
real world situations
Contents:
The Foundations of Entrepreneurship: What's Feeding the Entrepreneurial Fire? The Cultural
Diversity of Entrepreneurship. The Power of "Small" Business, The 10 Deadly Mistakes of
Entrepreneurship. Putting Failure into Perspective, How to Avoid the Pitfalls. Creativity, Innovation,
and Entrepreneurship, Creativity—A Necessity for Survival. Creative Thinking, Barriers to, Creativity
How to Enhance Creativity, The Creative Process. Techniques for Improving the Creative Process
Protecting Your Ideas.
Introduction to Innovation, Where does Design-Thinking Fit in? The Human Aspects, Thinking
Process, Visualization, Journey Mapping, Mind Mapping, Value Chain Analysis, Brainstorming,
Customer Co-creation and Learning Launch, Assumption Testing, Rapid Concept Development,
Rapid Prototyping, Leveraging Design Thinking for Innovation
Text Books

- Thomas W. Zimmerer and Norman M. Scarborough: Essentials of Entrepreneurship and
Small Business Management (IV Edition), Prentice Hall, 2004.

- C. Tripathi, P. N. Reddy: Principles of Management Tata McGraw Hill, 4th Edition, 2010.
- Poornima M Charantimath : Entrepreneurship Development - Small Business Enterprises ,
